Billing architecture · module dependency audit

Every module, and what it takes to sell it separately

All 69 nav-registered modules, traced for cross-module imports, shared tables, and foreign keys, so pick-your-own-modules billing doesn't sell someone a dead button. Complete inventory first, dependency-safe bundles second, a starting subscription structure last.

Before anything else

Module enablement today is a client-side route guard only — ModuleGate hides the nav item and redirects the route. Nothing in the database or in any library function checks whether a module is paid for. A "Convert to Proposal" button will still insert a real row into proposals even if that module isn't purchased — the row just becomes permanently unreachable until someone buys access to it. Billing enforcement needs to go deeper than reusing this mechanism; the map below tells you where those deeper checks are needed, not what happens today.

03How the bundles chain together

  1. Foundational — always on for every plan, including free.
  2. Operations Core — the first paid bundle almost everything else needs.
  3. Resourcing and Expenses & Travel — either or both, no order between them, both need Operations Core.
  4. Procurement & Inventory — independent of the above, needed before Finance or Pre-Sales.
  5. Finance & Billing Ops — needs Operations Core; fully powered up also wants Procurement & Inventory + Resourcing.
  6. Pre-Sales — needs Finance & Billing Ops + Procurement & Inventory.
  7. Sales CRM — needs Pre-Sales.
  8. Marketing — needs Sales CRM.
  9. Performance Reviews and Recognition & Awards — independent of everything above; Reviews optionally enhances Awards' scoring, in either order.
  10. HR & Resources — needs Resourcing.

04Recommendation

Sell at the bundle level (11 SKUs above), not the individual module level. A true per-module toggle across 69 items will eventually let someone buy Sales without Pre-Sales, or GRN without Purchase Orders — both dead ends today.

  1. Confirm the bundle boundaries above — especially B10 (Recognition & Awards), the one bundle independently sellable despite sharing a nav section with Performance Reviews.
  2. Add real enforcement before launch: an org's enabled-bundle set needs to gate at the RLS/RPC layer, not just ModuleGate's route redirect.
  3. Pick a chain-selling rule: auto-include a bundle's hard requirements in the cart, or block selection with an inline explanation of what's missing.

05Proposed subscription structure

A hybrid that keeps what's already built (billing_plans: Free/Starter/Pro/Enterprise seat tiers, STEP107) and adds true à-la-carte on top.

Seat tier governs how many people can log in, exactly as today. Bundle add-ons govern which modules those people can use, sold as flat monthly/yearly add-ons independent of seat tier. Enterprise keeps its current "all modules included" meaning; every other tier starts with Foundational only.
